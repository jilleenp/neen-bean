More Empty

by Jilleen Phillips
published on August 19, 2026

"It's awfully empty in here," Luke notes. "Well, the two of us make it a little less empty!"

He sits at one of the tiny boothes in the airy break room, his lunch in a shiny plastic carton. I contemplate my disintegrating left-overs in the microwave.

"You're right," I say brightly. I always try to be bright with Luke. He needs reassurance, support, recognition as a person of value. At least, that's what I imagine he needs. I have no idea what he actually needs.

Luke is unique. Eye contact is challenging, and he is likely to say things that don't make a whole lot of sense to others. He thinks he's funny, and maybe he is, but most of us don't get the joke. His teammates, comprised primarily of that esteemed cohort of "demanding inclusion" Gen Zers, don't know how to react to him, and point out that he "makes noises" that are distracting. They ask to have their desks moved away from them.

The microwave beeps-an awful sound. I pop the door and pull out my food, steamy and still cold in spots. "Well," I say brightly, "Enjoy your lunch!" I am not proud of this.

Luke bends over his salad and says nothing.

I leave the break room a little more empty.
